Each answer is one short call, and a case is ten to twenty of them.
How it plays
  • One of them did it, alone. Loretta tells you what, where and when, and what was found there.
  • Tap a face and ask. Type anything, or tap a question. They answer one thing at a time, and every question you ask is one off your count.
  • Whoever did it will lie to you. The others may lie about themselves, because people have business of their own. Only the guilty one lies about anybody else.
  • So a person is in the clear when somebody else puts them away from it: somewhere else when it happened, or somewhere else when the trace was picked up. Either the one saying so is honest, or they are the one.
  • Your notes write themselves. When someone says where they were at ten, it goes on the notes page at once, in a grid of who says they were where and when, with who else has put them there. When two accounts do not fit, put one to the other from that page: with real proof in front of them a liar changes their story. A bluff does nothing.
  • Name one. Right is good. Airtight is better: every other person in the room put in the clear by your notes. Three stars is airtight in close to the fewest questions it could have taken, and the case tells you afterwards what that shortest way was.
  • Tonight's case is the same for everybody, once a day. The casebook keeps your record, and who you have had in the chair before.

The code decides who did it, who was where and who is lying. The people only talk.
