Each seat is its own call and knows only what that person knows. A whole game is roughly 100 to 200 short calls.

How it plays

  • Knives know each other. Each night they take one person out of the game.
  • Town is everyone else. One of them is the Witness, who learns each night whether one person is a Knife.
  • Each day the table talks, then votes. Most votes is put out and their role is shown; a tie puts out nobody.
  • Town wins when every Knife is out. The Knives win when they are at least as many as everyone else.
  • No two nights the same. With the house dealing, a few wild cards are shuffled in, different every night: the Watch guards a door, the Bouncer throws one person out, the Listener hears how many Knives sit beside them, two Old Friends know each other, the Hothead takes someone along, the Drunk only thinks they are the Witness, the Turncoat joins the Knives if they come calling, and the Fool wins alone by being voted out. You are shown the deck when you sit down.
  • The house deal also sets one house rule for the night (a note left by the dead, a tie that puts out both, a secret ballot, the lights out on who the dead were, someone the house vouches for), and gives you a side bet of your own to play for.
  • The house keeps your tab: nights played and won, your streak, the cards you have held and the bets you have won. The people you have sat with remember the last time.
  • Whoever is voted out gets their last words before their role is shown. Listen to them.
  • The table talks on by itself: say something whenever you like. Tap a face, or what someone said, to aim at them; the quick replies say it in one tap (It's you. Liar. Why? I'm with you. Not me.), or type your own.
  • The talk ends when you call the vote, when most of the table says it is ready to, or when it gets late.

The code deals the roles, counts the votes and decides who is out. The people only talk, lie and vote.
